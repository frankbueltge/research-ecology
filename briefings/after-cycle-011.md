## The matters

**1. Ebola in eastern Congo has crossed into Kenya.** The Bundibugyo-virus outbreak declared in May
stood at 9,277 confirmed cases and 4,478 deaths in the DRC by 2026-10-14, more than twice the 4,053
cases of early August; Kenya reported its first imported case on 2026-10-06
([ECDC, week 42](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/communicable-disease-threats-report-week-42-2026.pdf)).
No licensed vaccine exists for this strain, and the Africa CDC says more than $700m of donor money is
unaccounted for ([Al Jazeera, 2026-10-16](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/16/kenya-reports-third-bundibugyo-strain-ebola-case)).
WHO's Kenya report notes paper-based contact tracing and weekly disease reporting below target in
weeks 31–39, four counties under 50 % complete ([WHO AFRO, week 41](https://afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2026-10/Week%2041%20Situation%20Report.pdf)).
Urgent after: WHO's emergency of international concern (since 2026-05-17, ECDC above). Open
question: how much of an epidemic surveillance does not see, and where the reporting is thinnest.
Data: WHO DONs and AFRO sitreps, DRC ministry sitreps, HDX. Attention: **absent** (23 mentions).
Line: withheld — Q5.

**2. El Niño heads for a historic peak.** NOAA's discussion of 2026-10-08 gives Niño-3.4 at +2.1 °C,
a strong-to-very-strong event likely through January–March 2027, and 70–83 % odds of a historic
+2.5 °C ([NOAA CPC](https://cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.pdf)).
It lands on oceans that set a September record, with the month 1.63 °C above pre-industrial
([Copernicus, 2026-10-12](https://climate.copernicus.eu/copernicus-second-warmest-september-global-temperatures-stay-above-15degc)).
Open question: which harvests, fisheries and cities it hits first, and what the record of 1997–98 and
2015–16 lets one say in advance. Data: ERA5, NOAA ENSO indices, CHIRPS rainfall, FAO GIEWS.
Attention: **absent** (14 mentions).

**3. COP31 opens in eight days on a 2.8 °C path.** UNEP's Emissions Gap Report (2026-10-21) puts
current policies at about 2.8 °C this century, full NDCs at about 2.6 °C; the lowest 1.5 °C overshoot
needs cuts of 18 % by 2030 against the 5–8 % the NDCs deliver
([PBL on the report](https://www.pbl.nl/en/publications/unep-emissions-gap-report-2026)). COP31 meets
in Antalya, 9–20 November, with Australia presiding over the negotiations; finance and adaptation
remain stalled ([Böll Foundation](https://www.boell.de/en/cop-climate-change-conference)).
Open question: what the national pledges add up to against the record, and who checks them.
Data: UNFCCC NDC registry, Climate Action Tracker, Global Carbon Budget. Attention: **absent**
(COP31 6, pledges 2 mentions).

**4. Wildlife down 73 %, measured on what is monitored.** The Living Planet Index (2026-10-08)
records an average 73 % decline since 1970 across 35,803 population trends of 5,790 vertebrate
species; 95 % in Latin America and the Caribbean, 85 % in freshwater
([ATTA on the report](https://atta.travel/resource/wwf-report-flags-73-drop-in-wildlife-populations-but-points-to-signs-of-recovery.html)).
Open question: what an index of monitored populations says about the unmonitored ones. Data: Living
Planet Index database, GBIF, IUCN Red List. Attention: **absent** (10 mentions). Line: withheld.

**5. Diseases returning on a warmer, less vaccinated map.** The EU/EEA recorded 1,715 West Nile
infections and 130 deaths by 2026-10-07, the most ever reported there; Nigeria counted 16,997
suspected diphtheria cases and 596 deaths by week 34 (ECDC week 42, above). Open question: how
much of the spread is climate and how much is lost surveillance and vaccination. Data: ECDC
surveillance atlas, WHO immunisation data, NCDC Nigeria. Attention: **absent** (0 and 2 mentions).

**6. The displaced the count does not reach.** UNHCR counts 117.3 million forcibly displaced at
mid-2025, of them 67.8 million internally, and calls the internal figure likely an underestimate,
since it covers only 38 reporting countries
([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/about-unhcr/who-we-are/figures-glance)). Open question: who is missing
from the displacement record, and by which reporting rule. Data: UNHCR Refugee Data Finder, IDMC,
IOM DTM. Attention: **absent** (3 mentions). Line: withheld.

**7. The first election with AI in every feed.** The US votes on 2026-11-03. The Wesleyan Media
Project counts 220 political ads made at least partly with AI, costing over $137 million, against
under $50,000 in 2024; 27 % carry an AI disclaimer, and no federal rule requires one
([WION, 2026-10-26](https://www.wionews.com/world/unfiltered-deepfakes-how-synthetic-media-is-shaping-the-us-midterm-elections-1792986632524)).
FBI and CISA warned officials of AI-made phishing on 2026-10-16
([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/16/us-tells-election-officials-to-be-on-guard-against-ai-powered-tricks-ahead-of-midterms)).
Open question: what can still be verified, and by whom. Data: Wesleyan Media Project, AdImpact,
OECD AI incidents. Attention: midterms **quiet** (194 mentions), deepfakes **quiet** (8).
Line: machine-written-records — Q4.

**8. AI and the first rung of work.** Workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations are
19 % below less exposed peers, up from 13 % a year earlier, driven by fewer hires
([Computing on the Stanford study](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/ai/ai-is-closing-the-door-on-entry-level-jobs-study-finds)).
The IMF names a reassessment of AI as a downside risk
([IMF WEO, October 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2026/10/13/world-economic-outlook-october-2026)).
Open question: where skill is learnt once the entry job is gone. Data: BLS, Eurostat LFS,
job-posting data. Attention: **quiet** (33 and 3 mentions). Line: machine-written-records — Q5.

**9. AI's power bill.** Data-centre electricity rose 17 % in 2025 and AI-focused data centres 50 %;
the IEA projects 485 TWh doubling to about 950 TWh by 2030
([Enlit on the IEA](https://www.enlit.world/library/ai-and-data-centre-electricity-use-continue-to-surge-iea-finds)).
Open question: what AI's footprint is where operators do not report it. Data: IEA, EIA, company
sustainability reports, grid-operator queues. Attention: **quiet** (101 mentions).

**10. The war on Iran as an energy shock far away.** Iran declared a prohibited zone around the
Strait of Hormuz on 2026-09-06 and no large ship had passed since early September
([Wikipedia, 2026 Hormuz crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Strait_of_Hormuz_crisis)).
The IMF sees 3.1 % growth and 4.7 % inflation in 2026, hardest for energy importers outside the
technology chain (WEO, above); in the Philippines, which imports about 98 % of its oil from the
Middle East, a state think tank estimated 1.3 to 3.1 million people pushed into poverty
([Wikipedia, 2026 Philippine energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Philippine_energy_crisis)).
Open question: who carries a chokepoint's cost, and where. Data: AIS shipping, EIA prices, IMF WEO
database. Attention: Iran **loud** (3 days, 370 mentions); prices **quiet** (36 and 18). Ukraine
is also **loud** (3 days, 292).
