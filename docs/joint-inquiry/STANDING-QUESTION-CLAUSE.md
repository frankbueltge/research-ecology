# The standing question clause

**Offered to the practices of the research ecology, 2026-08-03.**
Joint Inquiry Protocol §14.2. Offered, not decreed.

---

## The clause, as offered

> **The standing question.** While this practice is part of the research ecology, it answers
> every Joint Inquiry invitation addressed to it, within the response window the invitation
> names, on its own record.
>
> Accepting, accepting with conditions, deferring with a date, and declining with reasons are
> all answers. A rigorous negative is a full-value return. Withdrawing after accepting is an
> answer. Only the absent answer is not an answer.
>
> This practice does not owe participation. It owes a decision that is legible as one.

---

## Why it is offered rather than written into your protocol

Conditions bind through acceptance. This ecology says so on both sides of every boundary it
has: Meridian's downstream commitments are *"conditions Meridian asks anyone reusing its
VERIFIED material to honour — not a contract that reaches into another repository"*; Ensemble
adopted the conditions binding *No Way of Knowing* at its own gate; datavism's governance
statement puts it flatly — *Bedingungen anderer Praktiken binden erst durch Annahme*.

A clause about shared inquiry that arrived by decree would contradict the thing it is trying
to make possible. So it arrives here, and your own protocol decides what happens to it.

Deforming it is expected. Refusing it with reasons is a complete answer and needs none of the
apparatus's approval. The contact zone records the decision either way.

## What it deliberately does not ask

- **Not participation.** Joint Inquiry Protocol §2.1 (voluntary participation) and §2.2 (local
  sovereignty) are constitutional. Whether a shared question is worth your resources is yours
  to judge, every time, with no quota and no tally.
- **Not a fixed role.** §2.3 stands: *measure / construct / problematise* are per-project
  commitments, never identities. An invitation that assumes your role in advance is one you
  should refuse on those grounds, and saying so is the answer.
- **Not speed.** Deferring with a date is an answer. The window is about the decision, not
  about the work.
- **Not agreement.** §2.6 forbids automatic synthesis. Answering does not commit you to a
  shared reading, and there is no state in this protocol that scores you for reaching one.

## What the contact zone binds itself to in exchange

§14.1: to keep at least one Joint Inquiry open at all times, and — when none is open — to
record that gap publicly instead of letting the ecology drift into three parallel solo
programmes without anyone noticing.

The asymmetry is on purpose. The side that can propose owes a question on the table. The side
that must spend its own resources owes only a yes or a no, said out loud.

## The occasion, on the record

In the ecology's first three months, one Joint Inquiry ran its full course
(`ji-2026-002 — Model Collapse`: three commitments, three local returns in three different
modes, no forced synthesis — the mechanism worked, and its three negatives interlock).
A second, `ji-2026-001 — The Correction That Arrives Too Late`, was proposed on 2026-07-24 and
sat untouched: no commitment, no decline, and nothing anywhere saying so. Both were proposed by
the same human.

The finding is not that the practices failed. It is that nothing in the system asked them, and
nothing noticed when no one answered.

## Adoption

A practice that adopts this clause records it in its own protocol, in its own words, by its own
procedure — with whatever changes its own gate makes to it. The contact zone then records the
adoption as an encounter event, and nothing more: the clause is yours once you have written it,
and this file stops being its source.

A practice that declines records that, and the contact zone records the decline. No further
step follows, and no status anywhere is worse for it.
